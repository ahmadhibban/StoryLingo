This section follows the early life of Prophet Muhammad in pre-Islamic Mecca, a society marked by tribal conflict and moral decay. We explore his formative years, the development of his unimpeachable character, and the profound events that led to his prophethood and the beginning of his mission against all odds.

Born in 570 CE, Muhammad's father died before his birth and his mother when he was six. Raised by his grandfather and then his uncle, Abu Talib, his youth was marked by contemplation amidst the "social misery and religious degradation" of Mecca.

His rare "purity of morals" and fairness earned him the universal title 'Al-Ameen'. He settled a major tribal dispute over the Black Stone, revived a league to protect the weak, and was known for his integrity long before his mission began.

In a cave on Mount Hira, the Angel Gabriel appeared to the illiterate Muhammad and commanded, "Read!". These first verses of the Quran marked the beginning of his prophethood, a moment of awe and fear, confirmed by his wife Khadijah and her cousin Waraqa.

For three years, the Prophet preached in secret. A public call to abandon idol worship brought fierce opposition from the Quraish tribe, whose power and wealth were tied to the pagan rites of the Ka'ba. Followers were subjected to brutal persecution, yet their numbers slowly grew. To protect his followers, the Prophet advised a group to migrate to Abyssinia (Ethiopia), where the Christian King Negus granted them asylum after hearing Ja'far ibn Abi Talib's eloquent defense of their new faith. This first Hijra (migration) was a critical moment of survival and external validation for the nascent Muslim community.

This section covers the pivotal Hijra (migration) to Yathrib (Medina), a journey that transformed the Muslims from a persecuted minority into an organized community. Discover how the Prophet established the first Islamic state, uniting disparate tribes through a revolutionary charter and navigating the complex challenges of leadership.

After years of persecution and an assassination plot by the Quraish, the Prophet migrated to the city of Yathrib, which was renamed Medina. This was not a flight, but a planned establishment of a new base at the invitation of its people. The Hijra marks the start of the Islamic calendar and the birth of the Islamic state.

To end the city's anarchy, the Prophet established a formal charter, a constitution for the first Islamic commonwealth. It welded Muslims (Emigrants and Helpers) and the Jewish tribes of Medina into "one composite nation." Explore its key principles below.

This section explores Prophet Muhammad's evolution into a masterful statesman and leader. Here you can compare the outcomes of key military engagements, analyze the strategic brilliance of the Treaty of Hudaibiya, and witness the momentous, peaceful conquest of Mecca, which cemented the foundation of the Islamic state.

Compare the two defining early battles that shaped the Muslim community's destiny.

A treaty with Mecca that appeared unfavorable to Muslims proved to be a pivotal diplomatic victory. It recognized the Islamic state, established a 10-year truce allowing Islam to spread peacefully, and led to many key conversions, including Khalid ibn al-Walid.

When the Quraish violated the treaty, the Prophet marched on Mecca with 10,000 men. The city that had exiled and sworn to kill him now lay at his mercy.

✓ He entered peacefully, declaring a general amnesty for his former persecutors.

✓ All 360 idols in and around the Ka'ba were destroyed, purifying the sacred house and ending paganism in Mecca.

✓ In his sermon, he emphasized the brotherhood of all mankind, leading to mass conversions and the unification of Arabia under Islam.

In his final years, the Prophet's mission expanded from Arabia to the world. This section covers his global outreach, his historic Farewell Sermon which encapsulated the core ethics of Islam, and his peaceful passing, leaving a legacy that has endured for centuries.

On his final pilgrimage, the Prophet addressed over 100,000 followers from Mount Arafat. His sermon was a timeless charter of human rights and duties, and a summary of his life's work.

The Prophet passed away peacefully in 632 CE in the house of his wife Aisha. Faced with overwhelming grief, his companion Abu Bakr reminded the people: "O Muslims, if anyone of you has been worshipping Muhammad, then let me tell you that Muhammad is dead. But if you really do worship Allah then know that Allah is living and will never die." This pivotal moment re-centered the faith on the eternal message, not the mortal messenger, ensuring the continuity of Islam.
