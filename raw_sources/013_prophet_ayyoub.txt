Prophet Job was a descendant of a line of great prophets, with the most plausible account tracing his lineage back to Prophet Abraham (Ibrahim, pbuh). Allah Almighty mentions him among the progeny of Abraham, alongside figures like David, Solomon, Joseph, and Moses, highlighting his divinely chosen status.

Allah praised him directly in the Quran: "Truly! We found him patient. How excellent a slave! Verily, he was ever oft returning in repentance to Us!" (Ch 38:44). He was known for his immense generosity, constant remembrance of Allah, and profound thankfulness.

Driven by jealousy upon hearing the angels praise Job, Iblis (Satan) challenged the sincerity of Job's faith. He claimed Job's devotion was merely for his wealth and blessings. Iblis and his helpers destroyed all of Job's possessions—his cattle, servants, and farms—leaving him with nothing. Disguised as a wise old man, Iblis tried to sow doubt, questioning Allah's justice. Job's Unwavering Response: He fell into prostration and declared, "What Allah has taken away from me belongs to Him. I was only its trustee for awhile. He gives to whom He wills and withholds from whom He wills." His faith remained unshaken, founded on gratitude, not possessions.

Frustrated, Iblis argued that Job's true attachment was to his children. Given authority again, Iblis caused the house where Job's children lived to collapse, killing all of them. He then approached Job disguised as a sympathizer, again questioning Allah's reward for his piety. Job's Unwavering Response: He again prostrated and replied, "Allah sometimes gives and sometimes takes... Whether a thing is beneficial or harmful to me, I will remain firm in my belief and remain thankful to my Creator." His submission to divine will deepened.

In his final attempt, Iblis was granted authority over Job's body, but not his soul, intellect, or heart. He afflicted Job with a severe disease until he was reduced to "mere skin and bone," causing immense pain and leading friends and relatives to abandon him. Job's Unwavering Response: Despite excruciating suffering, he never complained. He patiently bore the hardship, remaining hopeful of Allah's mercy and continuously glorifying Him with his heart and tongue, the only parts of him left sound.

When his wife, worn down by years of hardship, momentarily despaired, Job gently reminded her of the context of their suffering. Throughout his long illness, when all others deserted him, Job's wife remained his sole comforter. She cared for him with unwavering loyalty for many years. However, exhausted by hardship, she was approached by Iblis and momentarily despaired, urging Job to call on Allah to end their suffering.

Job, recognizing Iblis's influence, was saddened by her weakened faith. In a moment of frustration, he swore an oath to punish her with 100 strokes if he ever regained his health. Later, when Allah restored him, he was faced with fulfilling his oath without harming his devoted wife.

A Merciful Resolution: Allah, in His infinite wisdom and mercy, provided a compassionate solution. He instructed Job: "Take in your hand a bundle of thin grass and strike therewith your wife, and break not your oath." (Ch 38:44). This allowed Job to uphold his promise to Allah while honoring the loyalty of his wife, a beautiful lesson in divine guidance and compassion.

In his state of utter helplessness, Job turned to his Lord. His prayer was not a complaint, but a humble and beautiful plea that stands as a model for all believers.

"Verily! Distress has seized me, and You are the Most Merciful of all those who show mercy."

Allah immediately answered his call. He commanded Job to strike the ground with his foot, causing a spring of cool, healing water to gush forth. He washed and drank from it, and his health was fully restored. Allah returned to him his family, and "the like thereof along with them"—doubling his blessings as a mercy and a reminder for all who worship Him.

The story of Prophet Job is a deep well of wisdom. It offers timeless guidance on how to navigate the trials of life with grace and faith. Click on each virtue below to explore the lessons embedded in his profound example.

Job's patience was not passive endurance but an active, conscious choice. He bore catastrophic loss and excruciating pain without complaint, always remaining hopeful of Allah's mercy. He teaches us that true patience is maintaining faith and gratitude, even when circumstances are at their worst, with a long-term perspective on Allah's blessings.

After every single loss, Job's immediate response was to prostrate in thankfulness. He saw all possessions as a trust from Allah, Who gives and takes as He wills. His story teaches that gratitude is not contingent on getting what we want, but is a fundamental state of a believer, acknowledging Allah's sovereignty in all situations, good or bad.

Job's faith was rooted in his heart, not his circumstances. Allah affirmed that his worship had "nothing to do with material things." The story powerfully illustrates that while Iblis could attack his body and possessions, he had no authority over his soul, intellect, or heart—the seat of true faith. This teaches that sincere belief is an internal fortress that cannot be breached by external calamities.

Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) shared an account that shows Job's character even in prosperity. After his restoration, while Job was bathing, a swarm of golden locusts fell on him. He began gathering them in his garment.

His Lord called to him, "O Job! Have I not made you too rich to need what you see?"

Job replied, "Yes, My Lord! But I cannot shun Your Blessings."

This beautiful exchange illustrates that true piety involves cherishing all of Allah's provisions, whether in adversity or abundance, and that a sincere servant remains perpetually humble and thankful for every divine favor.
