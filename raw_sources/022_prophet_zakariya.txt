Explore the profound narrative of Prophet Zakariyah (Zechariah) and his son Yahya (John). This journey reveals timeless lessons on unwavering faith, the power of sincere prayer, and the boundless mercy of the Divine. It is a testament to how hope can blossom even in the face of impossible circumstances.

This section explores the circumstances that led to Prophet Zakariyah's heartfelt prayer. It covers his personal character, his deep-seated concerns for his community, and the divine sign from Mary that ignited his hope for a seemingly impossible miracle.

Prophet Zakariyah was in his nineties, old and feeble, yet steadfast in his duties. His greatest sorrow was being childless, not for personal reasons, but because he feared his people would stray from divine law without a righteous leader to succeed him.

Despite his old age, he delivered sermons daily at the temple. He was charitable and always ready to help those in need. His primary concern was the continuity of spiritual guidance, fearing that after his death, "they would move away from Allah's teachings and change the Holy Laws to suit themselves."

A visit to Mary (Maryam) changed everything. He found her with fresh, out-of-season fruit provided miraculously by Allah. Her simple statement of faith opened his eyes to a profound truth.

When he asked about the fruit, Mary replied it was from Allah, asking, "Did he not know that Allah provides without measure for whom He wills?" This powerful reminder of divine omnipotence inspired Zakariyah to believe that his own miracle—a child in old age—was possible. He realized "nothing was impossible for his Gracious Lord!"

"My bones have grown feeble, and gray hair has spread on my head."

"I have never been unblest in my invocation to You, O my Lord!"

Fear for his people after him, requesting an heir to inherit "religious knowledge and Prophethood, not wealth."

"And make him, my Lord, one with whom You are Well-pleased!"

Here, we trace the divine response to Prophet Zakariyah's prayer. This was not just an answer, but a series of profound events, from the angels' call to the bestowing of a unique sign. Click on each step to see the details.

While he was standing in prayer, the angels delivered the divine message.

He was promised a son with a unique, divinely chosen name: Yahya (John).

He questioned how it was possible, given his extreme old age and his wife's barrenness.

The reply was simple and absolute: "It is easy for Me... I have created you before, when you had been nothing."

As confirmation, he was given a sign: he would not be able to speak for three nights, despite having no ailment.

"Then the angels called him, while he was standing in prayer in Al-Mihrab (a praying place or a private room)..."

This detail emphasizes that the divine response came during an act of worship, highlighting the connection between prayer and receiving divine blessings.

"'O Zakariyah! Verily, We give you the glad tidings of a son, His name will be Yahya (John). We have given that name to none before him.'"

The uniqueness of the name signifies the special status and distinct mission of the promised child.

"He said: 'My Lord! How can I have a son, when my wife is barren, and I have reached the extreme old age.'"

This natural human reaction underscores the truly miraculous nature of the event, making it more relatable.

"'So (it will be). Your Lord says, It is easy for Me. Certainly I have created you before, when you had been nothing.'"

A powerful reminder of divine omnipotence, linking this specific miracle to the ultimate act of creation itself.

"'Your sign is that you shall not speak unto mankind for three nights, though having no bodily defect.'"

This miraculous, temporary inability to speak served as an undeniable confirmation of the divine promise he had received.

Prophet Yahya was no ordinary child. He was blessed with wisdom, purity, and a profound mission from a young age. This visualization highlights the key attributes divinely bestowed upon him, painting a picture of his noble character.

Yahya's role was crucial. He was commanded, "O John! Hold fast to the Scripture (The Torah)," and was sent as a Prophet to confirm "the Word from Allah"—testifying to the miraculous creation of Isa (Jesus), son of Maryam.

The story of Zakariyah and Yahya is narrated in the Quran. The two primary accounts are found in Surah Maryam and Surah Al-Imran. While they recount the same core events, each provides unique details and perspectives. Use the tabs below to explore the foundational texts.

(Ch 19:1-15 Quran): This chapter details Zakariyah's secret invocation, his expression of feebleness, and his fear for his relatives. It highlights his request for an heir of religious knowledge. It records the glad tidings of Yahya (John), a name unique to him, Zakariyah's questioning, and the divine response emphasizing the ease of creation. The verses conclude with the sign of silence and a description of Yahya's divinely gifted wisdom, purity, righteousness, and dutifulness.

(Ch 3:38-41 Quran): This account notes Zakariyah's prayer for "good offspring." Here, angels call to him while he is praying, giving glad tidings of John and specifically mentioning his role in confirming the "Word from Allah" (Isa/Jesus), his nobility, and asceticism. It reiterates Zakariyah's questioning and the divine response, "Thus Allah does what He wills." The sign of silence is specified as three days, with a command to remember and glorify the Divine.

This timeless story offers profound spiritual insights that remain relevant today. It is a powerful reminder of key principles of faith.

True faith persists even when faced with circumstances that seem impossible by human standards.

A humble, secret, and heartfelt prayer is a powerful connection to the Divine, Who is the "All-Hearer of invocation."

Allah's mercy and power are boundless, providing "without measure" and transcending the laws of nature.

Steadfastness in devotion and duty, like Zakariyah's daily sermons despite his age, is a hallmark of righteousness.
