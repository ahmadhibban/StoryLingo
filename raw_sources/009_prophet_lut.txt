This section introduces Prophet Lot (Lut) and the divine purpose behind his journey to the city of Sodom. Explore his noble lineage, the nature of his calling, and the core message he was entrusted to deliver to a society steeped in corruption.

Prophet Lot, the nephew of Prophet Abraham, was sent by Allah to Sodom, a city on the western shore of the Dead Sea. His mission was to call its people away from their profoundly evil ways and guide them back to righteousness.

"Will you not fear Allah and obey Him? Verily! I am a trustworthy Messenger to you. So fear Allah, keep your duty to Him, and obey me." - Quran 26:161-163

He sought no worldly reward, emphasizing that his mission was purely for the sake of Allah, the Lord of all creation.

Despite facing continuous torture, ridicule, and complete rejection from his people, Prophet Lot remained patient and steadfast. He suffered greatly, both in public and within his own home, yet never wavered in delivering his divine message for many years. His story is a profound lesson in perseverance against overwhelming adversity.

Here, we delve into the character of the society Prophet Lot was sent to guide. This section visualizes the specific transgressions that defined Sodom, from widespread crime to the open practice of acts contrary to the divine and natural order.

The city of Sodom was described as being "filled with evil." Its inhabitants were known for a set of grave transgressions that created a state of total moral collapse.

Violence & Criminality: They would waylay, rob, and even kill travelers, making the city a place of terror for outsiders.

Sexual Immorality: They openly and unashamedly practiced what the Quran calls an "unnatural act," where men would engage in sexual relations with other men instead of with their wives.

Defiance of God: They were completely deaf to Lot's preaching, mocking his warnings and even challenging him to bring forth Allah's punishment.

Appearing as handsome men, the angels were sent to test the people of Sodom and execute Allah's judgment. Their arrival exposed the depths of the city's corruption.

Witness the divine retribution that befell Sodom. This section describes the cataclysmic punishment that erased the city and its people from the earth, a powerful and final response to their unrepentant corruption.

A violent earthquake rocked the town, so powerful it was as if the entire city was lifted and flung down in one jolt.

The towns were "turned upside down" and a storm of "stones of baked clay" rained down upon them, making destruction absolute.

The torment overtook the inhabitants precisely at sunrise, fulfilling the divine decree to cut them off in the early morning.

"So We saved him and his family, all, except an old woman (his wife) among those who remained behind. Then afterward We destroyed the others. We rained on them a rain of torment. How evil was the rain of those who had been warned." - Quran 26:170-174

Everyone and everything in the city was destroyed. The names of Lot's people and their towns were erased from the face of the earth, serving as an enduring sign for all who would come after.

The story of Prophet Lot is not merely a historical account; it is a source of timeless moral and theological guidance. This final section synthesizes the key takeaways from the narrative, highlighting its enduring significance for believers.

The narrative serves as a stark warning against severe immorality and the defiant rejection of divine guidance. The fate of Sodom illustrates the severe consequences of persisting in transgression.

Prophet Lot's unwavering perseverance in the face of immense suffering and hostility is a profound example of steadfastness in faith. It teaches believers to remain firm in their mission despite adversity.

The fate of Lot's wife demonstrates that salvation is based on personal faith and actions, not family ties. Even being the wife of a prophet could not save her from the consequences of her own disbelief and betrayal.

The Quran explicitly states that this story contains "signs for those who see" and "a sign for the believers." The destruction of Sodom is a perpetual lesson, and its location on a known trade route served as a physical reminder of divine justice.
