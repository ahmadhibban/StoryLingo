This section explores the story of Maryam (Mary), her noble lineage chosen by Allah, and the miraculous events surrounding the conception and birth of Prophet Isa. Her piety, devotion, and the divine signs given to her set the stage for one of history's greatest miracles, underscoring Allah's absolute power to create as He wills.

Allah chose the family of Imran for a special purpose. Maryam's mother, Hannah, vowed to dedicate her child to Allah's service. After her birth, Maryam was placed under the care of the Prophet Zakariyah. She grew up in the temple, devoted to worship. A sign of her special status was the miraculous provision of out-of-season fruits in her chamber, which she explained came directly "From Allah. Verily, Allah provides sustenance to whom He wills, without limit."

"Allah chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham and the family of Imran above the Alamin (mankind and jinns) (of their times)." (Qur'an 3:33). "So her Lord accepted her with goodly acceptance. He made her grow in a good manner and put her under the care of Zakariyah. Every time he entered Al-Mihrab to (visit) her, he found her supplied with sustenance. He said: 'O Mary! From where have you got this?' She said, 'This is from Allah.' Verily, Allah provides sustenance to whom He wills, without limit." (Qur'an 3:37)

While in seclusion, the Angel Gabriel appeared to Maryam as a man, announcing the gift of a righteous son. Astonished, she questioned how this could be, as she was chaste. The angel replied, "So (it will be), your Lord said: 'That is easy for Me (Allah)... and it is a matter (already) decreed'." Overwhelmed, she retreated and gave birth alone. In her distress, a voice instructed her to shake a dry palm tree, which miraculously provided fresh dates, and a rivulet appeared at her feet. This was a clear sign of Allah's care and her purity.

"She said: 'How can I have a son, when no man has touched me, nor am I unchaste?' He said: 'So (it will be), your Lord said: 'That is easy for Me (Allah): And (We wish) to appoint him as a sign to mankind and a mercy from Us (Allah), and it is a matter (already) decreed (by Allah).''" (Qur'an 19:20-21)

When Maryam returned to her people with the baby, they accused her of a terrible sin. Following divine instruction, she remained silent and pointed to the child. In a profound miracle, the infant Isa spoke, declaring: "Verily! I am a slave of Allah, He has given me the Scripture and made me a Prophet; And He has made me blessed wheresoever I be, and has enjoined on me prayer, and Zakat, as long as I live... Peace be upon me the day I was born, the day I die, and the day I shall be raised alive!" This miraculous event silenced the accusers and immediately established his true status as a servant and Prophet of Allah.

He [Jesus] said: "Verily! I am a slave of Allah, He has given me the Scripture and made me a Prophet; And He has made me blessed wheresoever I be, and has enjoined on me prayer, and Zakat, as long as I live. And dutiful to my mother, and made me not arrogant, unblessed. And Salam (peace) be upon me the day I was born, and the day I die, and the day I shall be raised alive!" (Qur'an 19:30-33)

This section details Prophet Isa's divine mission: to call people back to the pure worship of one God, to teach the inner spirit of the law, and to live a life of piety. It covers the incredible miracles he performed by Allah's permission as signs for humanity, and the staunch opposition he faced from religious authorities who feared losing their power and position.

Prophet Isa's message was a reinforcement of the Law of Moses, calling people to understand its spiritual essence. He taught a nobler life, focusing on the soul and inner being. He invited everyone to worship Allah alone, with no intermediaries, and denounced materialism and hypocrisy. He explained the Ten Commandments in their deeper meaning, such as the idea that even a lustful glance constitutes adultery of the eye. His core call was to focus on the eternal hereafter, not the fleeting distractions of the world.

Prophet Isa performed astounding miracles, always explicitly "by Allah's Permission." These were not displays of personal divinity but signs to prove his prophethood and demonstrate Allah's omnipotence. Sadly, the source notes that later followers misunderstood the essence of these miracles and began to worship him as a god. His miracles included:

Creating a living bird from a figure of clay.

Knowing what people ate and stored in their homes.

Receiving a table spread with food from heaven for his disciples.

"And (I come) as a messenger to the Children of Israel (saying): 'I have come to you with a sign from your Lord, that I design for you out of clay, as it were, the figure of a bird, and breathe into it, and it becomes a bird by Allah's Leave; and I heal him who was born blind, and the leper, and I bring the dead to life by Allah's Leave. And I inform you of what you eat, and what you store in your houses. Surely, therein is a sign for you, if you are believers.'" (Qur'an 3:49)

The religious priests of the time saw Prophet Isa as a threat. His call to worship Allah alone undermined their authority and exposed their corruption. They were angered by his disregard for their superficial rules, such as when he challenged their interpretation of the Sabbath. His most famous confrontation involved an adulteress they brought for stoning. Isa responded, "Whoever among you is sinless can stone her," teaching a profound lesson about judgment and mercy. When their attempts to discredit him failed, the authorities, aided by the betrayal of the apostle Judas Iscariot, plotted to have him killed by the Roman state.

This section addresses the most fundamental aspect of Islam: Tawhid, the absolute Oneness of Allah. It presents the Quranic refutation of the Trinity and any form of divine partnership or sonship. It also clarifies the definitive Islamic account of the crucifixion, which differs fundamentally from the Christian narrative and reinforces the theme of Allah's supreme power and protection of His messengers.

The cornerstone of Islam is the absolute, indivisible Oneness of Allah. The Qur'an describes the claim that Allah has a son as a "monstrous thing" so grave that the heavens are nearly torn apart by it. Allah is the Self-Sufficient Creator, who has no wife, no offspring, and no partners. He is beyond all human comparison. The most definitive statement of this is in Surah Al-Ikhlas: "Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, Nor is there to Him any equivalent."

The Qur'an states unequivocally that Prophet Isa was not crucified. This is a major point of divergence from Christian belief. The Qur'an explains that Allah protected his messenger from his enemies. It states: "...they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but [another] was made to resemble him to them... For certain is that they did not kill him. Rather, Allah raised him to Himself. And ever is Allah Exalted in Might and Wise." This event demonstrates Allah's ultimate power; while humans plot, "Allah is the best of planners." Isa was saved and ascended to heaven, and his story is a testament to divine protection, not human sacrifice.

"And [for] their saying, 'Indeed, we have killed the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, the messenger of Allah.' And they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; but [another] was made to resemble him to them. And indeed, those who differ over it are in doubt about it. They have no knowledge of it except the following of assumption. And they did not kill him, for certain. Rather, Allah raised him to Himself. And ever is Allah Exalted in Might and Wise." (Qur'an 4:157-158)
