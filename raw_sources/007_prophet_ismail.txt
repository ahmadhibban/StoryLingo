The story of Prophet Ishmael begins not with him, but with a profound act of faith by his parents, Prophet Abraham and Hajar. Commanded by God, Abraham led his wife and infant son to a desolate, uncultivated valley in the Arabian Peninsula—a place with no water, food, or life. This section explores the immense trust and obedience required to undertake such a journey, laying the foundation for the divine events that would follow.

Abraham is divinely instructed to take Hajar and their nursing son, Ishmael, on a long journey, leaving them in a barren valley with minimal provisions.

Bewildered, Hajar asks Abraham why he is leaving them. When she understands it is a command from Allah, her fear turns into a declaration of profound faith: "We are not going to be lost, since Allah Who has commanded you is with us."

Before leaving, Abraham prays to Allah, asking Him to make the land a place of prayer, to fill the hearts of people with love towards his family, and to provide them with fruits. This prayer foretells the sacred future of the site, which would become Mecca.

Left alone in the desert, Hajar and Ishmael's provisions quickly ran out. Faced with her son's agony from thirst, Hajar's maternal desperation fueled a frantic search for water. This section visualizes her tireless effort, an act of human perseverance that was met with divine grace, culminating in a miracle that would sustain generations and become a central rite of the Hajj pilgrimage.

Hajar's Seven Runs: A Testament of Hope

After her seventh run, a desperate Hajar heard a voice. An angel appeared and struck the ground, causing the miraculous spring of Zam-zam to gush forth. An angel reassured her, "Don't be afraid of being neglected, for this is the House of Allah which will be built by this boy and his father."

The miracle of Zam-zam transformed the barren valley into a viable place for settlement. This section explores how a community formed around this sacred well and how Prophet Ishmael grew within it. It also delves into a powerful lesson from Abraham on the importance of character in a household, conveyed through the metaphor of a house's threshold.

Upon visiting, Abraham found Ishmael's first wife complaining about their hardship and destitution. He left a message to "change the threshold," which Ishmael understood as a command to divorce her due to her lack of gratitude and contentment.

"Keep firm the threshold of his gate."

On a later visit, Abraham met Ishmael's second wife. She expressed gratitude to Allah, stating they were prosperous and well-off. Abraham's message was to "keep the threshold firm," signifying she was a blessed and suitable partner. Her gratitude attracted divine blessings.

The lives of the prophets are marked by profound trials designed to test and affirm their faith. Abraham faced the ultimate test: a divine command in a dream to sacrifice his beloved son, Ishmael. This section recounts this "manifest trial," focusing on the complete submission of both father and son, and the divine mercy that followed.

"O my father! Do that which you are commanded. InshAllah (if Allah wills), you shall find me of the patient."

Just as Abraham was about to carry out the command, having demonstrated complete submission, Allah called out to him. He had fulfilled the vision. The trial was complete. Allah ransomed Ishmael with a great sacrifice—a ram—an event commemorated annually by Muslims in the festival of Eid al-Adha.

The culmination of this divine narrative is the establishment of a physical and spiritual epicenter for monotheism. This section covers the collaborative building of the Kaaba by Abraham and Ishmael. It also provides an interactive tool to explore the subtle yet enriching differences between the historical narrations of these events, offering a deeper look into the story's transmission.

Commanded by Allah, father and son worked together to raise the foundations of the House of God. This act symbolizes the intergenerational transmission of faith and shared responsibility.

Throughout, they prayed: "Our Lord! Accept this service from us, verily, You are the All Hearer, the All Knower."
