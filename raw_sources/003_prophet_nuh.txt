This section explores the heart of Prophet Nuh's mission: the clash between his call to worship one true God and his people's deep-rooted idolatry. On one side, explore the logical, nature-based arguments Nuh presented. On the other, see the prideful and dismissive resistance of the disbelievers and interact with the idols they revered.

His core message was a clear warning: "Worship none but Allah," and abandon all false gods, cautioning of a "painful Day" if they persisted.

He pointed to the balance of night and day, the sun and moon, and the Earth itself as undeniable proof of a single, Divine Creator.

He argued that idol worship was not just a spiritual error, but a "suffocating injustice to the mind," which stifled human reason and true understanding.

He urged them to seek Allah's forgiveness, promising blessings of rain, wealth, children, and gardens as a reward for their return to faith.

The elite mocked Nuh, "You are only followed by the poor, the meek and the worthless." They demanded he dismiss his humble followers before they would even consider believing.

Prophet Nuh's mission was one of incredible, almost unimaginable, patience. For nearly a millennium, he called his people to truth. This interactive timeline highlights the key phases of his prolonged struggle, showing how rejection became an inherited tradition passed down through generations.

When all arguments failed and Allah revealed that no others would believe, judgment was passed. This was not a punishment of anger, but a necessary cleansing of an earth corrupted by incurable disbelief and wickedness.

Nuh was commanded to build an ark, far from the sea. His people mocked him relentlessly: "Are you going to drag it to the water or is the wind going to carry it for you?" He replied with certainty of the coming torment.

The sign came not from the sky, but from a domestic oven, from which water gushed forth. Nuh gathered the few believers, his family (except his disbelieving wife and one son), and pairs of every animal.

Water surged from every crack in the earth as torrential rains fell from the sky. The world was consumed by waves "like mountains," a force no human structure could withstand.

Nuh made a final plea to his son, "Embark with us!" But his son, trusting his own strength, replied, "I will betake myself to a mountain." A wave came between them, and he was among the drowned, a lesson that salvation is by faith, not bloodline.

After the flood, the survivors disembarked onto a purified earth, beginning a new chapter for humanity grounded in faith. Prophet Nuh's final advice to his sons distills his 950-year mission into timeless principles for all generations.

On his deathbed, Noah gave his sons "far reaching advice," a perfect summary of his life's purpose. He gave them two powerful commands to uphold and two grave warnings to heed.

Believe that "there is no god but Allah," a statement so weighty it would outweigh the seven heavens and earths.
