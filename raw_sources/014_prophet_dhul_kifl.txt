Dhul-Kifl is a profound figure mentioned twice in the Glorious Qur'an. In both instances, he is listed among revered prophets, which forms the basis for scholarly discussion about his exact status. This exploration delves into the Quranic texts, the interpretations of his name, his exemplary qualities, and the historical theories surrounding his identity, offering a comprehensive understanding of his legacy.

"And [mention] Ishmael and Idris and Dhul-Kifl; all were of the patient. And We admitted them into Our mercy. Indeed, they were of the righteous."

His inclusion with established prophets like Ishmael and Idris, and the shared attribute of "patience" (Sabr), strongly suggests a high spiritual station.

"And remember Our servants Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob - those of strength and [religious] vision... And remember Ishmael, Elisha and Dhul-Kifl, and all are among the chosen."

Here, he is again listed in a lineage of major prophets, described as being "among the chosen," reinforcing the perception of his prophethood.

The central discussion surrounding Dhul-Kifl is whether he was a prophet (Nabi) or a pious, just man (Wali/Salih). Who was Dhul-Kifl? Scholars have explored various linguistic and historical connections to understand his identity more deeply. The most prominent theory links him to a major Biblical prophet. This section explores the meaning of his name and the compelling evidence for his identification.

His name is an honorific title, not a personal name. Derived from the root K-F-L (ك-ف-ل), it carries several related meanings that reflect his character:

➤ The Possessor of a Portion/Double Recompense: Suggests he received a great reward for his deeds or shouldered a great burden of responsibility.

➤ The One with Responsibility/Guarantee: Reflects his role as a just leader who guaranteed the welfare and rights of his people.

➤ The One Firm to His Promise: Earned through his steadfastness in upholding his vows, especially when tested by Satan.
